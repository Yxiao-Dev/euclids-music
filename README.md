# Euclid's Music

### One rule of modular arithmetic, presented twice: once as harmony, once as rhythm.

**[Play it](https://yxiao-dev.github.io/euclids-music/)** · one HTML file · no install · works on a phone

![The full instrument: a pitch ring, a rhythm ring, two sliders, a shape catalogue, and a twelve-key piano.](assets/hero-full.png)

The whole instrument is above. Two sliders, two rings, and a piano that never retunes.

![The shape produced by every step at N = 5, 7, 12 and 19. Each row is a divisor lattice drawn as polygons, and each row reads the same forwards and backwards.](assets/shape-atlas.png)

Above is every shape the instrument can draw, at four different divisions of the octave. Nothing was designed. Each figure is the orbit of one step, and the rows are a picture of a theorem: when N is prime every step reaches every tone, so only stars appear; when N is composite the divisors interrupt, and hexagons, squares, triangles and a single line drop out of the sequence. Each row is also a palindrome, because k and N−k generate the same subgroup. The twelve-note row is the one a pianist recognises. Its five distinct shapes are the five symmetric chords of Western harmony, and they are there because 12 = 2²·3 and for no other reason.

Every sound and every shape on this page follows from one line:

```
next = current + k   (mod N)
```

Add a fixed step, wrap around, repeat. In algebra this is a walk in the cyclic group ℤ/N. In music it is a chord, a scale, and a groove. The two descriptions are of the same object. I built this instrument to hold them in one window, because I did not fully believe they were the same until I could hear one while watching the other.

## 1. The orbit and its length

Start at 0 and keep adding k. The walk must return, because ℤ/N is finite. The number of tones it visits first is

```
L = N / gcd(k, N)
```

which is the order of k in ℤ/N. The set it visits is the cyclic subgroup generated by k, and that subgroup equals the one generated by gcd(k, N). So the walk cannot produce an arbitrary collection of notes. It produces a subgroup, and ℤ/12 has exactly six of them, one for each divisor of twelve.

Five of those six already have names in music theory. That coincidence is the subject of this page.

![Six orbits drawn on the ring: the twelve-pointed star, the pentagram, the hexagon, the square, the triangle, and the line.](assets/classic-shapes.png)

| step k | gcd(k,12) | L = tones | polygon | musical name |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1, 5, 7, 11 | 1 | 12 | 12-pointed star | chromatic set; +7 traverses it as the circle of fifths |
| 2, 10 | 2 | 6 | hexagon | whole-tone scale |
| 3, 9 | 3 | 4 | square | diminished seventh |
| 4, 8 | 4 | 3 | triangle | augmented triad |
| 6 | 6 | 2 | line segment | tritone |

Read the middle column downward and it is the divisor lattice of 12. Read the last column downward and it is a first-year harmony syllabus. The page never states this. You recover it by moving one slider from +1 to +11 and listening to what closes when.

Two consequences are worth stating precisely, because both are audible.

**Inverse steps give congruent shapes.** Since 5 + 7 ≡ 0 (mod 12), the steps 5 and 7 generate the same subgroup and trace the same twelve-pointed star in opposite directions. The circle of fourths and the circle of fifths are therefore one object walked two ways, not two objects. The Reverse control performs exactly this involution.

**Transposition count equals the gcd.** The transpositions of a chord are the cosets of its subgroup, so their number is the index [ℤ/12 : ⟨k⟩] = gcd(k, 12). The augmented triad has four, the diminished seventh three, the whole-tone scale two. Messiaen catalogued these in 1944 as the modes of limited transposition and built a compositional language on them. In the language of this page, he was describing cosets.

## 2. The same algorithm, heard as rhythm

Euclid's procedure for the greatest common divisor is repeated subtraction. Applied to m pulses and n slots it yields the maximally even distribution of m onsets in n positions. This is Bjorklund's algorithm, and it coincides with taking onsets at ⌊i·n/m⌋ for i = 0…m−1, that is, the same walk as above with the non-integer step n/m and rounding.

Maximal evenness has a sharp consequence. The gaps between onsets take **at most two values**, ⌊n/m⌋ and ⌈n/m⌉, and the number of long gaps is exactly the remainder n mod m. For n = 16:

| m | division | gap multiset | name |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 16 = 3·5 + 1 | four 3s, one 4 | bossa family |
| 6 | 16 = 2·6 + 4 | two 2s, four 3s | tresillo ×2 |
| 7 | 16 = 2·7 + 2 | five 2s, two 3s | samba family |
| 4 | 16 = 4·4 + 0 | four 4s | four-on-the-floor |

The remainder is the musical content. When it is zero the gaps are uniform and the rhythm is square. When it is not, the leftover forces an uneven distribution, and that unevenness is what we call a groove. The caption under the slider prints the division with its remainder.

![Pulses at 5: the caption reads 16 = 3×5 + 1, and the bossa pattern appears.](assets/controls-bossa.png)

Both rings therefore execute one procedure under different parameters. On the pitch ring, gcd determines the cardinality of a chord. On the rhythm ring, the same gcd determines where the beats fall. Under the machine's description there is no distinction between harmony and rhythm. Under perception there is nothing but distinction. The instrument exists to make that gap available to a listener.

## 3. Twelve is a choice, and the keyboard is the evidence

Euclid states the gcd algorithm in Book VII of the *Elements*, around 300 BC. Bjorklund derived his version in 2003 while timing neutron pulses at a spallation source; Toussaint observed in 2005 that the resulting patterns are the traditional rhythms of much of the world. Zhu Zaiyu computed twelve-tone equal temperament to nine decimal places in 1584, in Ming China, on an abacus. These are unrelated problems. Each is a division that leaves a remainder.

It follows that N need not be twelve. The **Divisions** control retunes the instrument to 5, 7, 12, 19, or 31 equal divisions of the octave. Every structure in section 1 survives, because each depended only on gcd(k, N) and never on the value of N. The piano does not survive. It is a physical object with twelve keys in fixed positions.

![The keyboard at 12 divisions and at 19. The coloured marks are the tuning; the grey ticks are the keys. At 19 they no longer coincide.](assets/keyboard-12-vs-19.png)

In N-EDO the best approximation to a just ratio r is the nearest integer number of steps, ⌊N·log₂r⌉. Against a just fifth of 701.96 cents and a just major third of 386.31 cents:

| N | best fifth | error | best major third | error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12 | 7 steps, 700.0 | −2.0 | 4 steps, 400.0 | **+13.7** |
| 19 | 11 steps, 694.7 | −7.2 | 6 steps, 378.9 | −7.4 |
| 31 | 18 steps, 696.8 | −5.2 | 10 steps, 387.1 | +0.8 |

![The theory panel opened at 19 divisions. Every note name carries an arrow because none of them is exact, the keyboard beneath is inert, and the approximation errors are printed in cents.](assets/theory-19.png)

Twelve purchases a nearly exact fifth at the cost of a major third almost fourteen cents sharp. I have played that third daily since I was four and was trained to hear it as correct. Nineteen corrects the third and degrades the fifth. Thirty-one corrects both and does not fit under ten fingers. Away from twelve the note labels acquire arrows, since the pitch now lies above or below the key it is named for. The instrument computes these quantities at runtime and the test suite asserts them.

## 4. Verification

One file. SVG, CSS, Web Audio. No build step, no dependencies.

```
node tests/test-logic.mjs                 # 485 assertions, no dependencies
npm i jsdom && node tests/smoke-dom.mjs   # 127 assertions, boots the page headless
```

The logic suite asserts L = N/gcd(k,N) for every step at every supported N, the canonical Euclidean rhythms E(3,8), E(5,16) and E(7,16), maximal evenness for every pulse count, tuning (A4 = 440 Hz at N = 12, octave closure at every N), the ring geometry, and the cents table above to within 0.05 cents. The DOM suite boots the page, sweeps both sliders end to end, changes N, and confirms that the ring rebuilds, the step range follows, the tuning strip is correct, and the keyboard becomes inert away from twelve. A Chromium run over every N, step, and pulse count with audio playing reports zero page errors.

## 5. Limitations

No study with learners has been conducted. The design asserts that encountering a pattern before its name aids retention. This repository contains no evidence for that assertion. I do not know whether a first-time user reaches the structure unaided, or whether the visual channel is carrying the result while the audio is ornamental. The planned test is a short fixed task with the sample size fixed in advance, recording what users identify before opening the theory panel. The outcome will be reported here whether or not it favours the design.

The model is deliberately narrow. It uses a single twelve-key reference instrument, offers five temperaments, and says nothing about the provenance of a rhythm or its meaning to the people who play it. The mathematics describes a structure. It does not substitute for the history or for listening.

I do not claim novelty in modular pitch, Euclidean rhythms, or equal temperament. The contribution is the playable identification of the three.

## Run

Open `index.html`. Audio begins after the first tap on the centre control. Space toggles playback. Tap any dot or key to set the root. Reduced-motion settings are respected; the interface is fully keyboard-operable.
