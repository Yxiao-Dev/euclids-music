# Euclid’s Music

A modular pitch walk and an evenly distributed rhythm, heard side by side.

**[Play the instrument](https://yxiao-dev.github.io/euclids-music/)** · One HTML file · No build step

![The instrument with its pitch ring, rhythm ring, controls, and fixed twelve-tone reference keyboard.](assets/hero-full.png)

## Explore in thirty seconds

1. Press the centre Play button. Each rhythm pulse advances one pitch.
2. In twelve divisions, move **Step** to +4, then +3: hear a three-tone augmented triad become a four-tone diminished-seventh collection.
3. Change **Pulses** from 4 to 5. Four equal gaps become four three-slot gaps and one four-slot gap.
4. Tap a ring dot or a piano key to transpose the pitch collection.
5. Change **Divisions of the octave** to 19 or 31. The ring retunes; the piano remains a fixed twelve-tone reference. Tap the ring to select an exact pitch in these tunings.

The outer ring has sixteen equally timed slots, treated as sixteenth notes at 105 BPM. Pitch returns never insert pauses or change the selected root. The closing edge and label appear when playback actually returns to the starting pitch. Optional slot clicks make the time grid audible; an optional chord sounds the pitch collection at each return. Changing a control restarts playback from the selected root and slot zero. Stop silences scheduled voices. Leaving the page stops playback; press Play to restart when you return.

## What the two rings mean

The constructions are related through discrete division, but they are not the same algorithm.

### Pitch: addition modulo N

The inner ring repeatedly applies `next = (current + k) mod N`. Its first-return length is `L = N / gcd(k, N)`.

From pitch zero, the visited set is the cyclic subgroup generated by k. From any other root, it is a translate (coset) of that subgroup. A translated set need not itself be a subgroup.

At N = 12:

| Step k | Distinct tones | Path | Pitch collection |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 1, 11 | 12 | Regular dodecagon | Chromatic collection |
| 5, 7 | 12 | Twelve-pointed star | Same chromatic collection, in fourths/fifths order |
| 2, 10 | 6 | Hexagon | Whole-tone collection |
| 3, 9 | 4 | Square | Diminished seventh |
| 4, 8 | 3 | Triangle | Augmented triad |
| 6 | 2 | Line segment | Tritone |

Steps k and N−k traverse the same edges in opposite directions. Different steps can visit the same pitch set while drawing different paths: +1 and +5 are an example. The controls exclude k = 0, so the one-tone subgroup is not shown. For prime N, every allowed step visits all N pitches; this includes ordinary polygons as well as star polygons.

There are `gcd(k, N)` distinct transpositions of the pitch set. For example, the augmented triad has four distinct transpositions and the whole-tone collection has two.

All pitches sound within one octave, starting at C4. Ring direction describes pitch-class order, not a continuously rising or falling melody; crossing the octave boundary wraps the sounding pitch downward or upward.

### Rhythm: even distribution

Bjorklund’s algorithm distributes m onsets across n = 16 slots as evenly as possible. Consecutive onset gaps are `floor(n/m)` or `ceil(n/m)`. If the remainder is nonzero, exactly `n mod m` gaps have the larger size. If the remainder is zero, all gaps are equal.

| Pulses m | Division | Circular gap counts |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 16 = 4×4 + 0 | Four gaps of 4 |
| 5 | 16 = 3×5 + 1 | Four gaps of 3, one of 4 |
| 6 | 16 = 2×6 + 4 | Two gaps of 2, four of 3 |
| 7 | 16 = 2×7 + 2 | Five gaps of 2, two of 3 |

The two gap sizes need not strictly alternate. Neighboring-gap sizes alone are also not a complete test of maximal evenness: the distribution of those gaps around the entire circle matters. The logic tests check circular distances between every pair of onsets separated by the same number of onset steps.

The combined pitch-and-rhythm sequence repeats after `L / gcd(L, m)` complete rhythm bars. A pitch return can occur inside a bar; it does not reset or delay the rhythm.

This instrument presents onset patterns, not complete musical styles. It does not model the accents, instrumentation, performance practice, or cultural history that distinguish named rhythmic traditions.

### Tuning: twelve is one choice

The ring supports 5, 7, 12, 19, and 31 equal divisions of the octave. The frequency of degree i is `C4 × 2^(i/N)`. A just interval r is approximated by `round(N × log2(r))` steps.

| Divisions | Nearest fifth | Error from 3:2 | Nearest major third | Error from 5:4 |
|---|---|---:|---|---:|
| 12 | 7 steps, 700.0 cents | −2.0 cents | 4 steps, 400.0 cents | +13.7 cents |
| 19 | 11 steps, 694.7 cents | −7.2 cents | 6 steps, 378.9 cents | −7.4 cents |
| 31 | 18 steps, 696.8 cents | −5.2 cents | 10 steps, 387.1 cents | +0.8 cents |

Of these three tunings, twelve gives the closest fifth and thirty-one the closest major third. Thirty-one improves both approximations relative to nineteen, but its fifth is less accurate than twelve’s.

Outside twelve divisions, arrows indicate whether a pitch lies above or below its nearest twelve-tone key. C remains exact in every tuning. Keyboard highlights indicate nearest keys, not exact pitch matches. Changing divisions resets the root to C and preserves a best-fifth or best-third step when possible; other steps are scaled to the new division count.

## Design and scope

The project combines mathematical visualization, sound, and direct manipulation in a small browser instrument. Its contribution is the playable comparison, not a new algorithm or a claim that harmony and rhythm reduce to the same rule.

The design invites learners to encounter a pattern before reading its explanation. This is a design intention, not an established learning result: no learner study has been conducted. Automated tests check selected mathematical and implementation properties, not educational effectiveness or universal browser compatibility.

## Run and verify

Open `index.html` in a modern browser. Audio starts after a click or keypress. Space toggles playback when the page background or Play button is focused. Use native slider keys for the controls; arrow keys switch the octave-division radio buttons. Reduced-motion preferences are respected.

The page uses SVG, CSS, and Web Audio with no JavaScript runtime dependencies. Google Fonts is optional; system fonts are used when it is unavailable.

```sh
node tests/test-logic.mjs
npm install
npx playwright install chromium
npm test
```

The browser test checks the supported controls, actual scheduler timestamps and pitch returns, stop/start behavior, fixed roots, keyboard navigation, unsupported audio handling, reduced motion, and narrow-screen overflow. It also generates the README screenshot. These checks cannot substitute for listening on the devices used to view the project.

For GitHub Pages, deploy the main branch at the repository root. Keep `index.html`, this README, `LICENSE`, `package.json`, `tests/`, and `assets/` together.
